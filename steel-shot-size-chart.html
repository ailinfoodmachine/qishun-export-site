Steel Shot Size Chart and SAE Grade Selection

Steel shot size affects impact energy, coverage and the surface result. Select the grade together with hardness, equipment type and the required cleaning or peening outcome.

General selection logic

Buyer objectiveSelection directionConfirm before ordering
Heavy scale or robust castingsLarger shot is normally considered for higher impactEquipment capacity and acceptable profile
General cleaningBalance impact and coverageCurrent media, cycle time and dust level
Smaller or delicate partsFiner shot may improve coveragePart geometry and required finish

Do not specify size alone

Send the material being cleaned, contaminant, wheel or air-blast equipment, target cleanliness, surface profile and current media consumption. The supplier can then review grade, hardness and packaging together.

Quotation checklist

  • Requested SAE designation or particle range
  • Application: descaling, cleaning or shot peening
  • Workpiece material and dimensions
  • Hardness and durability requirement
  • Monthly quantity, bag size and destination

Frequently asked questions

Should steel shot size be selected by SAE grade alone?

No. Review the workpiece, contaminant, blasting equipment, target finish, hardness requirement and operating mix together with the requested size.

Is larger steel shot always faster for cleaning?

Not necessarily. Larger shot generally increases impact, while finer shot can improve coverage. The suitable balance should be confirmed with the part, machine and required surface result.

What information should a steel shot RFQ include?

Include the application, workpiece material and dimensions, current media, equipment type, required finish, requested grade or particle range, quantity, packaging and destination.
