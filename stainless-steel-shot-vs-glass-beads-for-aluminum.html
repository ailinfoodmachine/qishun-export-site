Aluminum Blasting Media Buyer Guide

Stainless Steel Shot vs Glass Beads for Aluminum Parts

Stainless steel shot and glass beads are both considered for cleaning and finishing aluminum parts. They are not interchangeable by default. The best choice depends on what must be removed, the approved appearance, the blasting machine and how the process will be controlled.

Short answer: start with the required surface, not the media name. Consider stainless steel shot when a reusable metallic medium and clean finishing are priorities. Consider glass beads when a spherical glass medium fits the cleaning or peening objective. For either option, confirm particle size and machine settings through a representative sample trial.

When stainless steel shot may fit the requirement

Stainless steel shot is supplied for clean finishing of aluminum parts, stainless steel castings and decorative hardware. Qishun's listed stainless options include 201, 304, 410 and 430, with wire-cut-and-rounded or atomized production routes represented across the stainless media range.

A buyer should specify which stainless material or manufacturing form is requested rather than writing only “stainless shot.” The correct choice also depends on the machine, the starting condition of the aluminum and the acceptance criteria. If the process is changing from another medium, ask the supplier to match the current particle specification or recommend samples for a controlled comparison.

Questions to settle before ordering

  • Is the objective cleaning, deburring, cosmetic finishing or peening?
  • What aluminum alloy, casting method and part geometry are involved?
  • Is the equipment air blast or wheel blast, and what media can it recover?
  • Is a specific stainless grade, particle size or conditioned shape required?
  • How will the finish be measured and approved?

When glass beads may fit the requirement

Glass beads are smooth, spherical media used for metal cleaning and peening. They are also described as chemically stable. For an aluminum application, the purchasing decision should connect the requested bead size to the finish target and to the equipment that will propel and recover the media.

Do not assume that every “bead-blasted” appearance is identical. Particle size, operating pressure, exposure, nozzle distance, bead condition and the original surface all influence the result. A useful sample approval records these variables so the production team can reproduce the accepted finish.

Avoid an appearance-only specification. A photo or phrases such as “satin” and “matte” can be interpreted differently. Pair the visual reference with a physical approved sample, a defined inspection method and any functional limits that matter to the part.

How to make the selection without guessing

1. Define the material to remove

State whether the incoming part has casting residue, oxidation, burrs, a previous coating or only an inconsistent cosmetic surface. The removal objective helps the supplier avoid recommending a medium that is either ineffective or unnecessarily aggressive.

2. Define the surface that must remain

List masked areas, threads, sealing faces, thin sections and critical dimensions. If the part will be coated, plated or assembled after blasting, include that downstream process in the inquiry. Media selection should support the finished component, not merely make the blasting step look successful.

3. Identify the equipment and operating limits

Tell the supplier whether the system is air blast or wheel blast and provide the current media specification when available. Cabinet recovery, separator settings and dust collection can affect whether a medium change is practical. Equipment compatibility must be confirmed before a bulk purchase.

4. Run a controlled sample trial

Use representative parts and hold unrelated variables steady. Label samples by media and operating condition. Inspect them with the people responsible for quality and the downstream process. Once a sample is accepted, retain it and record the settings as the purchasing and production reference.

5. Compare total process cost

Price per kilogram does not show the whole result. Review replenishment, cycle time, media recovery, dust handling, rejects, equipment wear and disposal. The most economical option is the one that repeatedly meets the part requirement at an acceptable total processing cost.

RFQ checklist for aluminum blasting media

  • Part name, aluminum alloy if known, dimensions and approximate unit weight
  • What must be removed and the required final appearance or surface condition
  • Air-blast or wheel-blast machine details and current operating media
  • Requested stainless grade or glass bead size, if already specified
  • Monthly quantity, preferred packing and trial-sample requirement
  • Delivery destination or port and expected purchasing schedule

Photos of the incoming part and the approved finish are useful, but they should not replace the written process requirements. If no media specification exists yet, say so clearly and request a sample recommendation.

Frequently asked questions

Can stainless steel shot be used on aluminum parts?

It can be considered for aluminum cleaning or finishing when the media grade, particle size, equipment settings and required surface are evaluated together. A representative trial is advisable before a production change.

Are glass beads suitable for aluminum blasting?

Glass beads are spherical media used for metal cleaning and peening. Their suitability for a specific aluminum part depends on the required finish, the material to remove, part geometry and the blasting equipment.

Which medium gives a smoother-looking finish on aluminum?

Both media can create a uniform finish under controlled conditions, but the result varies with particle size, pressure or wheel settings, exposure time and the starting surface. Approve a sample against a written finish standard instead of relying on the media name alone.

How should a buyer compare blasting media cost?

Compare the full process: purchase price, replenishment rate, cycle time, separation and recovery, dust handling, reject rate and disposal. A controlled production trial provides more useful evidence than price per kilogram alone.

What information is needed for a quotation?

Provide the aluminum alloy or part description, current media, equipment type, cleaning objective, target finish, requested particle size if known, monthly quantity, packing preference and destination port.

Need media samples or an export quotation?

Send the part, equipment, target finish, current media, quantity and destination. Qishun can review the request against its available stainless steel shot and glass bead products.
